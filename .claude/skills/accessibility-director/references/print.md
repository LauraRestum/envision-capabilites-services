# Print Accessibility

Applies to banners, postcards, flyers, one-pagers, name tags, table tents, signage, and every other physical piece. Print has no assistive technology layer, so the asset itself carries everything, and the alternate formats are part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.

## Color and type

- The hub's white-ground contrast table governs, verified against the actual stock (colored or kraft stocks shift ratios; when in doubt, proof and measure). Charcoal or Navy body text as the default; Green, Goldenrod, and Bright Blue never as body text on light grounds; Terracotta for large display type only.
- Type floors: 11pt body absolute minimum, 12pt preferred. Captions and credits 9pt minimum. Sentence case per brand; all-caps only for short letter-spaced display lines.
- Left-aligned body text; justified setting creates rivers that fight tracking for low-vision readers.
- No text over busy imagery without a solid panel or heavy scrim behind it.
- Meaning never by color alone: wayfinding, name tag role coding, and agenda tracks all pair color with a text label or icon.

## Legibility at distance

For banners and signage, size the hero line for the real viewing distance: roughly **1 inch of letter height per 10 feet**. A banner read from 30 feet needs 3 inch letters minimum for the essential line; verify the actual placement distance at the venue, not the mockup.

## Finish and stock

Matte or low-glare finishes over gloss wherever readability matters; event lighting on gloss erases text for everyone and especially for people with low vision. Adequate ink coverage on dark fills so large navy areas do not band.

## QR codes and the digital twin

- Every QR code is paired with a printed short URL, because a code alone excludes anyone who cannot aim a camera at it.
- QR minimum 1 inch square for arm's-length pieces, larger for posters; high contrast; full quiet zone.
- Every printed piece links (QR plus short URL) to an accessible digital twin: the web or tagged-PDF version of the same content. The print piece is one format of the content, never the only one.

## Alternate formats: the standing event rule

For event materials (the Supplier Summit, the White Cane Day Walk, H2F events, conference collateral), alternate formats are produced by default, not on request:

- **Large print edition**: 18pt minimum, 1.5 line spacing, single column, matte stock, produced alongside the standard run for agendas, programs, and menus.
- **Braille**: raised with the printer or a transcription service early; lead times run two weeks or more, so it enters the production schedule with the main run, and signage at registration says braille copies are available.
- **Digital**: the accessible twin live before the event, linked from confirmations.

For an organization whose events host people who are blind or have low vision as guests, speakers, and colleagues, this is table stakes; treat any event piece missing its alternate formats as unfinished.

## Piece-specific floors

- **Name tags** (the Avery 74541 flow): name at 24pt bold minimum, highest-contrast pairing on the stock, organization line smaller but at floor, no color-only role coding, low-glare laminate.
- **Table tents**: content on both faces, floors apply to both.
- **Postcards and flyers**: one clear CTA, QR plus short URL, floors apply even at small trim sizes; cut content before shrinking type.
