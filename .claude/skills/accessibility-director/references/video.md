# Video and Motion Accessibility

Applies to sizzle reels, motion graphic card sets, event videos, program stories, and every script and storyboard that precedes them. The governing idea: description is written into the script, not bolted on as a separate track later. Integrated description at the writing stage costs nothing; retrofitting a described version of a finished cut is expensive and usually skipped, which is how inaccessible video ships.

## Audio-complete scripts (the house standard)

Write every script so the audio alone tells the complete story. Narration and speakers naturally say what is happening on screen: "our team assembles the hand warmer kits on the Wichita line" over that footage gives a viewer who is blind the full picture with no separate description track ever needed.

**The listen-only test, run on every script and every rough cut:** play it with the screen off. If anything essential is missing (a name, a number, a place, an action, the call to action), the script is not done. This is the video equivalent of the keyboard walkthrough.

Rules that fall out of it:

- **On-screen text is spoken.** Every stat, name, title card, URL, and CTA that appears as text is also said aloud, or an equivalent is. A closing card with a URL gets the URL read.
- **No information by visuals alone.** If the edit relies on the viewer seeing a logo, a location, or a before-and-after, the audio carries it too.
- **Speakers are identified in audio** the first time they appear ("I'm Patrick, I lead operations"), not only by lower-third.

## Music-only and motion-card formats

Formats built on on-screen text over music (the sizzle-card style) default to one of these, in order of preference:

1. Add voiceover, making the cut audio-complete.
2. Produce an audio-complete alternate cut alongside the silent version.
3. If neither is possible, publish a full text alternative directly beside the video, and treat the video itself as supplementary, never the sole carrier of the message.

## Captions

- Every video, every platform, captioned. Auto-captions are a draft only; the ship version is human-reviewed for accuracy, punctuation, speaker identification, and meaningful sound cues ("[applause]", "[machinery hum]").
- Synced within a beat of the speech, inside platform safe zones, high-contrast (white on a dark scrim band), never covering essential visuals.
- Open (burned-in) captions for feed placements where platform captions are unreliable; closed captions where the platform supports proper tracks.

## Motion safety and on-screen text

- Nothing flashes more than three times per second. No strobe effects, ever. Transitions calm; the brand's restraint rules already point this way.
- On-screen text meets the contrast tables (add a scrim over footage) and stays legible at feed size: for essential text, roughly 40px or larger on a 1080 frame.
- Text stays on screen long enough to be read aloud twice.
- Meaning never by color alone in motion charts or graphics; label the series in audio and on screen.
- Dialogue and narration mixed clearly above the music bed; no essential information buried in low-volume audio.

## Delivery

- A full transcript (dialogue plus the visually essential context) published alongside every video; it serves screen reader users, deaf-blind users, skimmers, and search simultaneously.
- When footage arrives finished and cannot be made audio-complete, commission a separate audio description track as the fallback.
- Never autoplay with sound. Embedded players follow the web module's keyboard and control rules. Thumbnails get alt text.

## Verification

- [ ] Script passed the listen-only test before production; rough cut passed it again
- [ ] All on-screen text spoken or equivalently conveyed
- [ ] Captions human-reviewed, synced, and legible
- [ ] Flash and strobe check clean; on-screen text meets contrast and timing rules
- [ ] Transcript published with the video
- [ ] Music-only formats shipped with VO, an alternate cut, or a full text alternative
