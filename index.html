Mandatory source. Prime vendor. Base supply.

Envision is an active, pre-cleared source across federal and state channels. The qualification work is already done. Every channel below is live, and all pricing has been reviewed and approved.

Mandatory Source
AbilityOne
Envision is affiliated with the National Industries for the Blind. AbilityOne is the federal program that designates certain products as mandatory-source from approved nonprofit manufacturers. SKILCRAFT-branded Envision products in the federal catalog include trash bags, seal-closure bags, VISI-BELTs, and print services.
88% of Envision's combined Direct Labor is performed by people who are blind or have low vision. Exceeds AbilityOne's 75% threshold.
Prime Vendor
DLA Prime Vendor
Envision holds the AbilityOne Base Supply Center (ABOBSC) Tailored Logistics Support contract with DLA Troop Support — a $360 million, 5-year IDIQ. IDIQ stands for Indefinite Delivery, Indefinite Quantity, a federal contract type with a set ceiling and flexible ordering; every purchase over the micro-purchase threshold is competed among the 16 AbilityOne Base Supply Store Operators.
ABOBSC TLS: $360M, 5-year. $408M combined DLA capacity with H2F. Each offers customer-specific items within its individual scope.

Berry Amendment
Compliant. The Berry Amendment requires DoD to give preference to domestically sourced textiles and related end items. Envision's qualifying production, including ACU trousers, MOLLE pouches, and ETOOL carriers from Dallas, meets the domestic sourcing requirement.
TAA compliant
Trade Agreements Act. The Trade Agreements Act restricts federal procurement to U.S.-made or designated-country end products. Envision's federal-channel products satisfy TAA country-of-origin requirements.
Buy American Act
Compliant. The Buy American Act sets domestic content thresholds for federal purchases. Envision's U.S. manufacturing satisfies the applicable domestic content tests.
DCMA Alternate Release Dallas
Authorized under the DCMA Alternate Release Program. Qualifying military products ship on Alternate Release without prior government source inspection, reflecting high confidence in Envision's quality management system and saving weeks of audit time. Release is product-specific rather than blanket: some items, such as the ACU trouser, still go through source inspection at the treatment center.

AbilityOne Explained

A federal buying channel, and where Envision sits in it.

Where the program came from, and how authority flows from the President to the shelf. Open any panel for detail.

How it got here
Federal agencies were directed to buy specified products from nonprofit agencies employing people who are blind. Employment was the qualification from the first day: an agency earned the channel by doing the work, not by asking for relief. National Industries for the Blind was formed to coordinate supply across the participating agencies.
Products only NIB formed Employment is the qualification
Senator Jacob Javits amended the act to cover people with significant disabilities beyond blindness, and to include services alongside products. The qualification did not change, only who it covered: agencies still earned the channel through the people they employed. A second central nonprofit agency was added to serve the newly eligible agencies, and the customer base widened from supply items to full service contracts.
Services added Second central nonprofit agency Wider eligibility
The program runs today as AbilityOne, overseen by the U.S. AbilityOne Commission. Items and services on the Procurement List are a mandatory source for federal agencies, so a qualified buyer purchases them from a participating nonprofit agency instead of running a separate competition. The qualification is now a measured one: at least 75% of an agency's direct labor hours must be performed by people who are blind or have significant disabilities. The ratio is reported and audited, not assumed.
